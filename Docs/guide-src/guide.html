1Read this first: two hookups

The QMX and the Digirig Lite do the same job (getting audio between the phone and a radio), so you normally use one or the other, not both together.

The QMX doesn't need the Digirig Lite

The QMX has a 24-bit USB sound card built in. One USB-C data cable from the QMX to the iPhone carries receive and transmit audio. It has no analog line input for digital modes, so there's nowhere to plug the Digirig into for JS8. Out of the box the QMX only transmits when a computer sends it a CAT command, which an iPhone can't do. So you turn on the QMX's own Digi › VOX setting: then it transmits whenever audio arrives over USB, and the app just sends audio (its VOX PTT method).

Use the Digirig Lite with radios that have analog audio: a DATA/ACC jack or mic and speaker jacks (for example an FTDX3000 rear DATA port, an HT, or a Xiegu).

Setup A

QMX straight to the phone

  • PTT method: VOX – audio on both channels
  • Radio mode: Digi; QMX menu Digi › VOX = ON
  • Tune the QMX dial by hand to match the app
  • No CAT on iPhone (iOS blocks USB serial ports)
Setup B

Digirig Lite + analog radio

  • PTT method: Digirig Lite – PTT tone (right ch.)
  • Radio mode: USB / DATA-U, data input from the rear/ACC jack
  • JS8 audio on the left channel, keying tone on the right
  • The Lite has no serial port, so no CAT through it

2What you need

Lay everything out before you start. Check items off as you go.

Common to both setups

  • iPhone with JS8 Firefly installed (from Xcode or TestFlight). iPhone 15 or later has USB-C. On a Lightning iPhone, use Apple's Lightning to USB 3 Camera Adapter, ideally with a charger plugged into its pass-through port.
  • Your callsign and 4- or 6-character grid locator (for example EM73 or EM73tu).
  • A dummy load for the transmit tests, or a matched antenna you're licensed to transmit on.
  • Accurate time. Leave Settings › General › Date & Time › Set Automatically on. JS8 needs your clock within about ±1 second of UTC.

Setup A: QMX

  • QMX with a 9–12 V DC supply on the 2.1 mm barrel jack (USB doesn't power the transmitter).
  • USB-C to USB-C data cable. Some cheap cables only carry charging power and won't work; if the phone doesn't see the radio, try another cable first.
  • Recent QMX firmware (1.04 or later recommended; it shows a flashing E when Digi audio is missing or too low, which is useful for testing).

Setup B: Digirig Lite

  • Digirig Lite, revision 1.1 or later. Revision 1.2 (the USB-C version) is best: it has a PTT indicator LED and better tone keying.
  • The Digirig audio cable for your radio (TRRS on the Digirig end). Digirig sells ready-made cables for common Icom, Yaesu, Elecraft, Xiegu and Baofeng models.
  • A short USB-C cable between the Lite and the phone (or plug the Lite in directly if it fits).
  • Optional: a USB isolator or ferrites if RF gets into the audio (hum, PTT stuck on, the app restarting while you transmit).
Safety first

Do every transmit test into a dummy load or a matched antenna. Never key a transmitter with nothing on the antenna port. Transmitting needs an amateur licence; until you're ready, use TX Off or tap Just listen for now at first launch.

3App first run and bench test

Do this with no radio connected. It confirms the app, clock and audio pipeline work before any hardware is involved.

  1. Open JS8 Firefly. Enter your callsign and grid on the welcome screen and tap Get on the air. Allow notifications if you want alerts for messages sent to you. Allow microphone access when iOS asks; the app can't receive without it. Your callsign and grid show at the top left of the Live tab.
  2. Get to know the tabs. Live (waterfall + band activity), Chat (conversation), Stations (who you've heard), Inbox (stored messages) and Settings.
  3. Check the clock. Go to Settings›Time. UTC (corrected) should match a reference such as time.gov to within a second.
  4. Check audio with no interface plugged in. Go to Settings›Audio & PTT. It should say No USB audio interface — using built-in audio. Back on Live, talk or tap near the phone. The waterfall reacts to sound from the iPhone's microphone.
  5. Turn TX off for now. In the button row above the message box, tap TX On so it reads TX Off. You'll turn it back on for the transmit tests.

4Setup A: QMX direct to iPhone

One cable, no interface box. Work through receive first, then a transmit test into a dummy load.

4.1 Prepare the radio

  1. Connect the dummy load (or antenna) and the DC supply, then power on the QMX.
  2. Select Digi mode on the QMX.
  3. Turn on the QMX's VOX. In the QMX menu go to Digi › VOX and set it to ON (the default is OFF). Then in System config › CAT timeout, choose DISABLED; otherwise the QMX drops out of transmit after the timeout because no CAT command arrives. Without this step the QMX receives fine but never transmits from the iPhone.
  4. Set the dial to a JS8 frequency: 7.078 MHz (40 m) or 14.078 MHz (20 m) are the busiest. The QMX dial should match exactly what the app shows at the top right.

4.2 Connect and configure the app

  1. Plug the USB-C cable between the QMX and the iPhone. Settings›Audio & PTT shows USB audio interface detected, and Route names the QMX. If it doesn't, tap Restart audio at the bottom of that section, then try another cable.
  2. Set PTT method to VOX – audio on both channels. This matters: the Digirig tone setting would put a 1 kHz keying tone into the QMX along with the JS8 signal.
  3. Input channel: Left. If the waterfall stays blank later, try Mix.
  4. Rig Control › Connection: None. iPhone apps can't open the QMX's USB serial (CAT) port, so you set the frequency on the radio.
  5. Match the band in the app. Tap the dial at the top right and pick the same band you set on the QMX (for example 40m · 7.078.000).

4.3 Set receive level

  1. Tap Levels (or the thin level bar under the header).
  2. On a quiet part of the band, aim for about 30 dB, inside the green "Good" zone.

  3. Watch the waterfall. On an open band you'll see JS8 signals as short stacked bursts, and decodes appear in band activity within one or two time slots (15 s each in Normal).

4.4 Transmit test (into the dummy load)

  1. Turn TX On. Turn the iPhone volume all the way up and set TX level to about 90%. The QMX only keys once the audio is near the top of its range (its Digi › Rise threshold defaults to 80%).
  2. Open Levels and press Tune (key a steady carrier). The QMX switches to transmit and shows output power. The app shows TUNING.
  3. If the QMX shows a flashing E or doesn't transmit, first check Digi › VOX is ON on the QMX. Then make sure the iPhone volume is at maximum and raise TX level toward 100%.
  4. Press Stop tune, or close the Levels panel (Tune always stops when the panel closes).
The QMX has no ALC to watch

In Digi mode the QMX measures the frequency of the incoming tone and transmits one clean carrier at that frequency. Output power is set by the radio, not by audio level. Strong, clean audio is what it needs, so run TX level and the iPhone volume high; that doesn't add power or splatter.

With VOX on, any sound from the phone transmits

Notification sounds, keyboard clicks or music from another app would go out over the air. Turn on Do Not Disturb (or a Focus), switch off keyboard clicks, and don't play other audio while the QMX is connected.

5Setup B: Digirig Lite + analog radio

For radios with a DATA/ACC jack or mic and speaker audio. The most important step is proving the tone PTT keys the radio.

5.1 Prepare the radio

  1. Connect the dummy load or antenna.
  2. Mode: USB-DATA / DATA-U (or plain USB if the radio has no data mode). Turn speech processing off.
  3. Route data audio from the rear/ACC jack. Many radios have a menu for this (for example DATA IN select / DATA MOD source = REAR). Turn the radio's own VOX off; the Digirig keys the PTT line directly.
  4. Set the dial to 7.078 or 14.078 MHz.

5.2 Connect and configure the app

  1. Plug the radio cable into the Digirig's TRRS jack and the Lite's USB-C into the iPhone. The Lite's power LED comes on (rev 1.2), and Settings shows USB audio interface detected.
  2. PTT method: Digirig Lite – PTT tone (right ch.). The defaults are a good start:
    SettingDefaultNotes
    PTT tone1000 HzDigirig suggests 1 kHz. Higher (1500–2000 Hz) may key faster.
    Tone level70%Raise if PTT drops out or doesn't key.
    Swap channelsOffTurn on only if your cable or unit expects the tone on the left.
    PTT lead / tail150 / 100 msGives the relay time to close before audio starts.
  3. Input channel: Left (try Mix if there's no receive audio).
  4. Rig Control: None unless you have a Bluetooth LE serial adapter or a network rig server (rigctld / flrig). The Lite has no serial port.
Why tone PTT?

On a computer the Digirig Lite keys the radio from a GPIO pin on its sound chip. iOS doesn't let apps touch that pin, so the app plays a steady tone on the right audio channel instead. The Lite's VOX circuit hears that tone and pulls the radio's PTT line. Digirig calls this method experimental; it works best on board revision 1.2 and isn't wired up on revision 1.0.

5.3 Prove PTT keying (most important test)

  1. Turn TX On and set the iPhone volume to about 75%.
  2. Open Levels and press Tune. The Lite's PTT LED lights, the radio goes to TX and shows output power.
  3. LED lights but the radio doesn't transmit: check the cable and the radio's data-PTT settings.
  4. Nothing happens: raise Tone level toward 100% and the phone volume, then try a 1500 or 2000 Hz tone. Check the board revision (tone PTT doesn't work on rev 1.0).
  5. Radio stays in TX after you stop: lower the tail time, and check for RF getting into the cable (add ferrites or a USB isolator).

5.4 Set levels

  1. Receive: aim for about 30 dB on a quiet part of the band. The Lite's input level is mostly set by the radio's data/line-out level menu.
  2. Transmit: with Tune running, raise TX level until the radio's ALC just starts to move, then back off a little. Little or no ALC means a clean signal. If power doesn't rise as you go up, you've passed the clean point, so back off.
If the tone PTT won't stay reliable

Some radios (like the FTDX3000) can be keyed by CAT instead. On iPhone that means a Bluetooth LE serial adapter on the radio's CAT port, or rigctld / flrig running on a computer or Raspberry Pi on the same Wi-Fi. Then set PTT method to CAT / rig control command.

6First on-air test

Swap the dummy load for an antenna. Listen first, then make a short transmission.

6.1 Listen

  1. Pick a band that's open (20 m in the daytime, 40 m in the late afternoon and evening). Keep the speed on Normal, and leave Settings›JS8›Decode all speeds on.
  2. Wait two or three minutes. Callsigns appear in band activity and in the Stations tab, with SNR and offset.
  3. Check the timing. In Settings›Time, Median decode Δt should be within about ±0.5 s. If it's larger, tap Sync clock to received signals.

6.2 Transmit

  1. Pick a clear spot. Tap an empty part of the waterfall between about 500 and 2500 Hz to move your offset there.
  2. Send a heartbeat: tap HB. (Heartbeats normally go between 500 and 1000 Hz; the app takes care of this unless you turned on HB anywhere.) The period ring turns red and shows TX, and the radio transmits for about 13 seconds.
  3. Watch for replies. Other stations' heartbeat acknowledgements show up in band activity.
  4. Try a directed query. On the Stations tab, long-press a station you can hear well and choose Ask SNR?. If they hear you, they'll answer with a signal report.
  5. Have a QSO. Select a station, type a message (for example HW CPY? QRP 5W) and send. End with 73 or SK from the ⌘ menu.
Good habits
  • Leave Confirm auto-replies on at first, so you approve every automatic answer before it goes out.
  • Keep the iPhone volume where you set it during Tune. Changing it changes your TX drive.
  • Background receive keeps running when you leave the app, but the phone has to stay connected and powered. Long sessions drain the battery.

Pre-flight checklist

  • Antenna or dummy load connected, radio powered
  • Radio mode and dial match the app's band
  • QMX only: Digi › VOX = ON, CAT timeout disabled
  • USB audio interface detected in Settings › Audio & PTT
  • Correct PTT method for this hookup
  • RX meter in the green on a quiet part of the band
  • Tune test keys the radio; TX level set
  • Clock correct (Δt within ±0.5 s once decoding)
  • TX On
